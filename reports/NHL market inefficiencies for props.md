# Hunt prop unders and empty-net mechanics, not moneylines

No public study shows any NHL betting inefficiency surviving against Pinnacle closing prices. The large-sample evidence says NHL moneylines are among the best-calibrated sports markets, with no favourite-longshot bias in either direction. The only quantified edges in the research notes sit in player props at soft books. In those markets the margin is loaded on the overs, and model-filtered **unders on saves, shots on goal and assists** returned **+5.7% to +10.9%** over three seasons. Those results are self-reported, partly in-sample, and priced at the best available soft-book odds rather than at Pinnacle. Game-state mechanics give the most promising structural angle for game lines. **Empty-net goals have roughly tripled as a share of scoring in 20 years**, coaches still pull goalies about five minutes later than theory says they should, and overtime games always end on an odd total. Together these shape the joint distribution of margin and total in ways a naive Poisson model or a stale cover-rate table will misprice. For predicting players, the repeatable signal sits in volume and role: time on ice, power-play share, shot attempts per 60 and shots against. Percentages such as shooting %, on-ice Sh% and PDO are mostly noise. The pipeline should therefore test three things. First, prop unders driven by a volume model, measured against Pinnacle no-vig and closing line value (CLV). Second, favourite -1.5 and its correlation with the over, conditioned on the trailing coach's pull aggressiveness. Third, SOG and points lines that lag role changes. 2025-26 is the discovery season and 2026-27 the holdout.

## Sharp game-line markets show no exploitable structural bias

The strongest efficiency evidence is also the most discouraging for anyone hoping to beat NHL sides. Robbins studied **19,456 NHL games from 2007-08 to 2022-23**. Every odds bin returned a negative result. The heavy-favourite bin (−700 to −219) lost 2.91% and the longshot bin (+190 to +505) lost 4.13%, a difference with p = 0.546. The ANOVA for equal returns across bins gave p = 0.905. The calibration slope's 95% interval of [0.934, 1.01] includes 1, and Robbins calls NHL odds "the most efficient of the odds set we have examined" ([Robbins 2023](https://myweb.ecu.edu/robbinst/PDFs/Weak%20Form%20Efficiency%20in%20Sports%20Betting%20Markets.pdf)). Its roughly 3.3% margin points to consensus or market-average prices rather than Pinnacle. That makes the result conservative: a sharper price set would leave even less room. Older papers on a reverse favourite bias in hockey exist ([Woodland & Woodland 2001](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/j.2325-8012.2001.tb00385.x)), but the newer, longer sample overrides them. A paper on NHL totals asks whether there is an under bias ([Paul & Weinbach](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227410468_Market_Efficiency_and_the_NHL_totals_betting_market_Is_there_an_under_bias)), but the research team could not retrieve its numbers. Model-based results exist: Buttrey reported a significant positive return from a Markov goal and penalty model on moneylines ([Buttrey 2016](https://ideas.repec.Org/a/bpj/jqsprt/v12y2016i2p87-98n1.html)). One modeller reports **+11.7% ROI on 341 bets** in 2016-17 but says the same model would not win today because those edges are now priced in ([Betstamp](https://www.betstamp.com/education/How-I-Built-The-NHL-Betting-Model-That-Beat-The-Books)). A parallel prop backtest found no edge on full-game totals, with overs losing 4.0% and unders 4.4% ([MJACode PR #860](https://github.com/MJACode/betting-model/pull/860)).

The honest answer to "what persists against sharp prices" is therefore **nothing that has been documented**. No study the team found tests puck lines, home/away bias, popular-team bias or any prop market against Pinnacle closing lines. That gap is exactly why the pipeline's pick-time-versus-close data is valuable. CLV against Pinnacle is the only verdict worth trusting, and no published work has delivered one for NHL props or puck-line structure.

## Prop markets carry their margin on the overs

Player props are where the quantified edges cluster, and the pattern is consistent. For goalie saves, a study of **3,516 lines from 2023-24 to 2025-26** found that **94.63% of multi-book goalie-games had the identical number at every book**. In other words, books copy one number and compete only on price. The posted number averaged **25.35** against **24.51** realised saves, a 0.84-save over-shade, and the median margin was 6.48%. Even so, blind unders hit only 53.75% against a 53.49% break-even, which is within noise ([Nebula Insights](https://nebulainsights.net/blog/nhl-goalie-saves-props.html)). The bias is real but too small to bet blindly. It needs a model to filter it.

The one public backtest that filters it reports **saves unders +7.8% on 1,755 bets**, **SOG unders +5.7% on 4,707 bets** and **assists unders +10.9% on 1,063 bets**, all with 95% intervals above zero and stable by season. **Blind overs at DraftKings lost 9-12%**, against 0-3.5% for blind unders ([MJACode PR #860](https://github.com/MJACode/betting-model/pull/860)). Three caveats limit how far that carries to this pipeline. First, the author tuned design choices on the same seasons used for the results. Second, the saves model was over-confident, predicting 63% and delivering 57%. Third, results depended on line shopping: SOG ROI fell from +5.7% to +3.3% when restricted to DraftKings. A Pinnacle-only paper bettor gets no shopping benefit. If Pinnacle's prop margin is lower and its over-shade smaller, the edge could vanish entirely. Whether it does is the first question to answer.

The research found **no evidence on how quickly books reprice props** after goalie confirmations, power-play unit changes or line-rush news. Every source on that subject was unquantified explainer content ([MyBookie](https://www.mybookie.ag/sports-betting-guide/goalie-confirmations-and-line-movement/); [ESPN](https://global.espn.com/espn/betting/story/_/id/42470775/nhl-odds-betting-goaltending-factor)). One weak hint of stale pricing exists: SOG unders returned +7.2% in the opening weeks of each season, slightly better than the full sample ([MJACode PR #860](https://github.com/MJACode/betting-model/pull/860)). That is consistent with books leaning on prior-season numbers, but the sample is small.

## Volume and role predict props; percentages mostly do not

The stats that beat season averages are the ones built on many events and on deployment. **SOG per game is shots/60 × TOI** ([DobberHockey](https://dobberhockey.com/2024/12/22/geek-of-the-week-shot-attempts-shots-on-goal-and-shots-per-60/)), so projected even-strength and power-play TOI is the first input. Attempt rates pool more events than shots on goal. Attempt-based team metrics hold up better than scoring-chance metrics for that reason: R² of about 0.95 versus 0.83 in one model ([Betstamp](https://www.betstamp.com/education/How-I-Built-The-NHL-Betting-Model-That-Beat-The-Books)). On-net share (SOG divided by attempts) swings sharply between seasons even for established shooters. Forsberg went from 50.1% to 41.4% with a stable attempt rate, and Guenther from 53% to 37.1% ([DobberHockey](https://dobberhockey.com/2024/12/22/geek-of-the-week-shot-attempts-shots-on-goal-and-shots-per-60/)). That makes on-net share a noisy quantity to regress, not a skill to extrapolate. Shooting % takes about **223 shots to reach 50% signal**, roughly a full season for a heavy shooter ([Don't Tell Me About Heart](https://donttellmeaboutheart.blogspot.com/2014/12/how-long-does-it-take-for-forwards.html)). It is irrelevant to SOG and only weakly relevant to goals. The research found no citable games-to-stabilise figure for attempts per 60 (iCF/60) at game level, so the pipeline should estimate it with split halves on its own data.

For points, what matters is role, not hot streaks. Only **5 of 138 skater-seasons (3.6%) with on-ice Sh% above 11% repeated it** the next year ([JFresh](https://jfresh.substack.com/p/percentage-luck-in-hockey-explained)). On-ice Sh% explains roughly **0% of variance for defencemen and about 40% for centres**, and the recommended regression is about 67% toward the mean ([TSN / Yost](https://www.tsn.ca/examining-on-ice-shooting-percentage-by-position-1.338499)). Power-play share, by contrast, is concentrated and moves production immediately. Elite power-play defencemen take about **80% of their team's PP time**. Chychrun took over PP1 and scored 5 points in 7 games, and Vlasic had 4 points in 5 games while covering for an injured Seth Jones ([DobberHockey](https://dobberhockey.com/2024/12/13/frozen-tool-forensics-power-play-defencemen-and-changing-roles-for-spurgeon-ekblad-dunn-and-others)). Those are anecdotes, not a controlled test. For defencemen, individual expected goals per 60 (ixG/60) and the 5v5 points rate correlated about 0.20-0.25 with next-season points, while individual shot rate was inconsistent ([DobberHockey 2026](https://dobberhockey.com/2026/08/07/ramblings-finding-useful-stats-for-predicting-future-goal-and-point-production-among-defencemen/)).

For saves, the variance split points straight at shot volume. At 28 shots and a .905 save percentage, binomial noise on saves is only about ±1.55 saves (derived). Yet **46.7% of saves results land more than 5 saves from the line** ([Nebula Insights](https://nebulainsights.net/blog/nhl-goalie-saves-props.html)). That implies a total SD of roughly 6-7 saves, driven by shots against, game script and early pulls rather than goalie skill. Score effects quantify the script channel. Evolving-Hockey's 5v5 SOG weights for the home side run from **0.862 when down 3+ to 1.104 when up 3+**, which means trailing teams generate materially more shots ([Evolving-Hockey](https://evolving-hockey.com/glossary/score-adjustments/)). The goal weights stay within about ±7-9%, so score effects move shot props far more than goal totals. On the second night of a back-to-back, a goalie faces about **+0.8 shots** and his Sv% drops about 0.008 ([Hockey-Graphs 2014](https://hockey-graphs.com/2014/12/01/goaltender-performance-vs-rest/)). That is a net wash for saves, apart from a higher risk of being pulled.

## Empty nets and overtime reshape the margin-total distribution

Late-game mechanics are where game lines could still hide a structural edge, because the inputs keep drifting. Empty-net goals rose from **2.4% of goals in 2005-06 to about 7% in 2024-25**, running at about 0.40-0.42 per game ([Daily Faceoff](https://dailyfaceoff.com/news/how-empty-net-goals-have-changed-the-nhl); [Daily Faceoff, Mar 2025](https://dailyfaceoff.com/news/top-8-statistical-chase-nhl-stretch-drive-ovechkin-crosby-hellebuyck-makar)). 2021-22 set the record at 477 ([ESPN 2022](https://www.espn.com/nhl/story/_/id/34020506/why-2022-stanley-cup-playoffs-set-new-empty-net-goals-record)). In 2024-25, **74% of wins were one-goal games or two-goal games padded by an empty-netter**, the highest rate on record ([ESPN 2025-26](https://africa.espn.com/nhl/story/_/id/47210375/nhl-2025-26-games-shootout-theories-trends-stats-standings)). Theory says a team down one should pull its goalie with about 6 minutes left ([Asness & Brown via Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/when-to-pull-the-goalie-nyu-researcher-offers-risk-based-analysis)). Coaches actually pulled at about **1:04** in the 2022 regular season ([ESPN 2022](https://www.espn.com/nhl/story/_/id/34020506/why-2022-stanley-cup-playoffs-set-new-empty-net-goals-record)), so the drift toward earlier pulls has room to continue. Pull aggressiveness also differs by team: Toronto and Carolina were consistently early from 2013-20. Extra-attacker goal rates do not repeat (r ≈ 0), and shot-attempt rates repeat only weakly (r ≈ 0.19-0.22) ([Hockey-Graphs 2020](https://hockey-graphs.com/2020/05/18/the-state-of-goalie-pulling-in-the-nhl/)). Pull *timing*, a coach decision, is therefore the input most likely to persist. No public source has measured how well it repeats.

Two consequences follow for pricing. First, **favourite -1.5 and the over are positively correlated through empty-net goals**, most sharply at low totals. On a 5.5 total, a 3-2 game that becomes 4-2 into an empty net flips both the puck line and the total at once. The research found no measurement of whether same-game parlay pricing captures this. The only sources were generic sportsbook patents ([USPTO 11657680](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11657680)). Second, **every overtime game ends on an odd total**, because the winner is credited one goal. A 2-2 regulation tie becomes 5 and loses the 5.5 over; a 3-3 tie becomes 7 and wins a 6.0 over rather than pushing. The share of games reaching overtime held at 20.5-23.5% from 2016-17 to 2024-25, then jumped to **27.3% early in 2025-26** ([ESPN 2025-26](https://africa.espn.com/nhl/story/_/id/47210375/nhl-2025-26-games-shootout-theories-trends-stats-standings)). If that persists it lifts +1.5 underdog covers, because every overtime loss covers, and it cuts pushes on 6.0. A Poisson total without an explicit overtime step will misprice both. The rate may also be regression noise, so shrink it toward about 23% until 2026-27 confirms it. A betting-site claim that heavy favourites cover -1.5 only 40-45% of the time is unsourced and conflicts with ESPN's close-game figure, so it should be discarded ([Slapshot Diaries](https://www.slapshotdiaries.com/sports-betting-canada/guides/puck-line-explained.html)).

## Three patterns to test next

Each test below uses data the pipeline already holds. Each has a pre-stated success criterion: positive Pinnacle CLV and calibration against Pinnacle no-vig probabilities, not raw ROI alone. 2025-26 is used to fit models and 2026-27 to date is the holdout.

| # | Pattern | Construction | Primary test | Kill condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Volume-model unders on saves and SOG** | Projected shots against for the confirmed starter, from MoneyPuck team shots-for and shots-against logs, the opponent's shot generation and a score-script adjustment keyed to the Pinnacle moneyline (Evolving-Hockey weights). Projected SOG = projected TOI × regressed iCF/60 from play-by-play (shots + misses + blocks) × regressed on-net share. Bet the under when model P(under) − Pinnacle no-vig ≥ threshold, set on 2025-26 | (a) Does Pinnacle's posted number exceed realised saves or SOG on average, as soft books do (+0.84 saves)? (b) Holdout ROI and mean CLV (pick-time vs close) of flagged unders | Pinnacle over-shade ≈ 0 **and** CLV ≤ 0 on the holdout |
| 2 | **Favourite -1.5 and over, conditioned on pull aggressiveness** | From play-by-play, derive each coach's median pull time when trailing by one (first time the trailing team's goalie leaves with under 5:00 left in regulation, excluding delayed penalties), plus the favourite's empty-net conversion. Compute empirical P(-1.5 cover), P(over) and P(both) by moneyline band and total line | (a) Realised -1.5 cover minus Pinnacle no-vig -1.5, regressed on the trailing coach's pull aggressiveness. (b) Joint P(-1.5 ∩ over) vs the product of marginals at 5.5 and 6.0. (c) Overtime rate by moneyline and total band vs implied +1.5 pricing | Coefficient on pull aggressiveness ≈ 0 and joint ≈ product of marginals |
| 3 | **Role-change lag on SOG and points lines** | From NHL per-game skater stats, flag players whose PP TOI share over the last 3-5 games differs from their season share by ≥ 15 percentage points. Also flag players whose on-net share has fallen while iCF/60 holds steady. Fade point-streak players whose on-ice Sh% is above 10% with an unchanged role | Residual (actual − Pinnacle line) and CLV, by flag direction (promotion → over, demotion or on-ice-Sh% heater → under) | No difference in residual or CLV between flagged and unflagged players |

Pattern 1 checks whether the only documented prop edge exists at a sharp book at all. Its measurement is cheap: the mean of posted number minus realised result, by market, needs no model. Pattern 2 targets the mechanism that has changed most in a decade. It has the least public scrutiny and a persistent, measurable coach-level input. The play-by-play situation-code decoding behind it is unverified working knowledge, so validate it against shift-chart goalie-off times on a handful of games before trusting it. Pattern 3 tests the widely repeated but unproven claim that books price off season averages and lag deployment changes. The anecdotal PP1 examples and the early-season SOG result are the only support for it.

Statistical power is the binding constraint. About 1,300 games per season, times roughly 1.25 seasons, gives on the order of 1,600 favourite -1.5 observations. At a 40% cover rate the standard error is about 1.2 percentage points (derived), so only a deviation of 2.5 points or more will register. That is why Pattern 2 should lean on CLV and on the conditioning variable rather than on raw cover rate. The prop patterns produce thousands of player-games and can detect smaller edges. Pinnacle's prop limits and posting times are unknown from the notes, so log whether the price existed at pick time before counting a bet.

## Conclusion

The evidence reverses the usual betting intuition. The famous structural biases, favourite-longshot and home or popular teams, are dead in NHL sides, and the surviving edges are mundane: the over-shade books apply to props, and game-state mechanics drifting faster than pricing tables update. Every positive result so far was measured at soft books with line shopping. A Pinnacle-only paper pipeline will most likely find smaller edges than the public backtests claim, and it may find none. A null result in Pattern 1 would still be informative, because it would show the prop "edge" is soft-book margin rather than model skill.

The more original opportunity is Pattern 2. Coach pull timing is a human decision that analytics has criticised for a decade without changing much. It can be measured from free play-by-play, and it feeds directly into margin, total and their correlation at once. If any NHL inefficiency persists against sharp prices, it is most likely in markets whose fair price depends on those late-game decisions rather than on team strength.
